# Methodology

## 1. Research question

The Lab asks:

> Which avoidable insecurities prevent people from realising their desired family size, and which interventions improve freedom, security and wellbeing without reproductive pressure?

## 2. Concepts that must remain separate

- **Ideal family size:** abstract preference when obstacles are ignored.
- **Intended family size:** planned or desired number under real-life expectations.
- **Expected family size:** respondent forecast of likely final family size.
- **Completed fertility:** children ultimately born to a real cohort.
- **Period fertility:** synthetic annual measure based on current age-specific birth rates.

These measures cannot be subtracted mechanically or treated as the same population.

## 3. Evidence states

Every indicator uses one of these states:

- `verified_starting_fact`;
- `verified_context_indicator`;
- `verified_but_stale`;
- `data_gap`;
- `disputed`;
- `superseded`.

Every figure must show its population, time period, source and limitation.

## 4. Family Security dimensions

### Housing

Potential measures include housing-cost burden, overcrowding, search duration, residential stability and access to family-sized units.

### Income and work

Potential measures include income replacement, schedule predictability, employment continuity, promotion, earnings and pension effects by parenthood and parity.

### Childcare

Potential measures include unmet demand, usable opening hours, closure days, reduced-hours days, backup availability, quality and affordability.

### Care fairness

Potential measures include paid and unpaid work, solo-care time, mental load, parental-leave duration, perceived fairness and relationship wellbeing.

### Health

Potential measures include fertility-care access, maternity access, birth outcomes, patient-reported experience, postpartum physical and mental health and continuity of care.

### Relationships and community

Potential measures include time scarcity, partnership safety, social support, loneliness and practical help.

### Access to services

Potential measures include eligibility clarity, application time, processing time, non-take-up, appeals, digital exclusion and multilingual accessibility.

## 5. Policy-test standard

A policy test must specify:

1. target group;
2. barrier;
3. intervention mechanism;
4. leading metrics;
5. family outcomes;
6. cost and distributional effects;
7. pilot and comparison design;
8. risks and unintended consequences;
9. stop condition;
10. attribution boundary.

## 6. Causal standard

The Lab may say that an intervention **contributed** to an observed change only when the timing, mechanism, exposure and alternative explanations are documented.

Causal claims require stronger designs such as:

- randomised or phased implementation where ethical and practical;
- difference-in-differences with credible comparison groups;
- interrupted time series;
- regression discontinuity;
- matched longitudinal cohorts;
- pre-registered natural experiments.

A simple before-and-after fertility movement is insufficient.

## 7. Fairness standard

Every major finding must include:

- strongest reasonable counterposition;
- alternative explanations;
- groups that may be harmed by the framing;
- what evidence would weaken the claim;
- whether the policy improves choice and wellbeing even without increasing births.

## 8. Regional comparison gate

Regions are not ranked until:

- definitions match;
- data periods are aligned;
- population structure and case mix are described;
- missingness is visible;
- rural and urban access differences are handled;
- policy exposure is documented.

## 9. Privacy boundary

The public project uses aggregate, public-source information. It does not collect identifiable fertility histories, pregnancy records, medical files, partner details or employer allegations.

## 10. Success

The project succeeds when it produces:

- a reproducible family-security baseline;
- clearer public responsibility;
- testable interventions;
- independently reviewed results;
- visible corrections and failed hypotheses;
- measurable improvements in security, health, equality and family choice.
