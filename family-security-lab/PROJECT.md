# Family Security Lab — Product Definition

## Problem

Germany measures births well, but does not yet present one coherent public view of whether people can realise the family they want and which barriers prevent them from doing so.

The project treats low fertility as a **family-security and reproductive-agency problem**, not as a mandate to pressure people into parenthood.

## Product promise

For every region or policy proposal, the Lab should make five things clear:

1. **Need:** Which family-security gap is visible?
2. **Evidence:** What is verified, stale, missing or disputed?
3. **Responsibility:** Which institution can change it?
4. **Intervention:** What bounded action can be tested?
5. **Impact:** Which leading and family outcomes will determine whether it helped?

## Users

- prospective parents and families;
- citizens comparing local conditions;
- researchers and journalists;
- municipalities and Länder;
- family-policy and equality organisations;
- employers and public-service operators;
- reviewers checking definitions, fairness and causal claims.

## Core products

### 1. Family Security Index

Seven dimensions:

- housing;
- income and predictable work;
- childcare reliability;
- care fairness;
- reproductive, maternity and postpartum health;
- relationships, time, community and safety;
- access to benefits and services.

The index must never collapse these dimensions into a fertility leaderboard without validated weighting and comparability.

### 2. Evidence Map

A versioned registry of indicators with:

- exact definition;
- population and period;
- value and unit;
- evidence state;
- interpretation;
- limitation;
- official source.

### 3. Policy Test Matrix

Each intervention links:

```text
Barrier → mechanism → leading metric → family outcome → pilot → risks
```

Priority is not proof. A policy advances only when a problem baseline and credible measurement design exist.

### 4. Regional Pilot

A five-to-seven-region comparison selected for learning value, not for ranking. Initial candidates include Berlin, Sachsen, Niedersachsen, Hamburg, Nordrhein-Westfalen and one high-cost southern state.

### 5. Public Impact Log

The Lab separates:

- Reach impact;
- Evidence impact;
- Accountability impact;
- System impact;
- Family outcome impact.

A change in the birth rate is never automatically attributed to the project or one intervention.

## Release boundary

The project may publish public-source analysis and clearly labelled hypotheses. It may not:

- collect private fertility or medical records;
- pressure people to reproduce;
- rank people or regions by moral worth;
- present correlation as causation;
- automatically recommend benefits or medical treatment;
- claim a fertility effect without adequate follow-up and evaluation.
