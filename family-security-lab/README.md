# Family Security Lab

**Make the family people want possible — without pressure, blame or hidden costs for mothers.**

Family Security Lab is an independent public-interest project inside Digital Democracy Studio. It investigates which economic, time, health, housing, childcare and relationship conditions help people realise their desired family size safely and freely.

The project grew out of SafeTrace case **CAND-051 — Der Kinderwunsch im Wartestand**, but now has its own product identity, roadmap and data layer.

## Product promise

A citizen, policymaker or researcher should be able to answer:

1. Where is family formation becoming insecure?
2. Which groups carry the largest hidden costs?
3. Which public systems are responsible?
4. Which intervention is testable now?
5. Did security, choice and family wellbeing actually improve?

## North star

> **The share of people who can realise their desired family size without avoidable economic, health or gendered harm.**

This is not a state fertility target. Childfree lives, small families and reproductive autonomy remain equally legitimate.

## Current evidence

The initial Germany evidence map separates:

- ideal family size;
- intended family size;
- expected family size;
- realised family size;
- period fertility;
- housing security;
- childcare availability and reliability;
- employment and income effects;
- care equality;
- reproductive and maternal health;
- access to family benefits.

Current highlighted signals include:

- ideal family size among young adults reported near **2.31**;
- intended family size reported near **1.75**;
- Germany's period fertility at **1.32** in 2025;
- substantial childcare demand gaps in the best available national analysis;
- a large employment gap between mothers and fathers of children under three;
- strongly unequal planned parental-benefit duration.

These indicators are not mechanically interchangeable and do not prove that one policy caused or prevented births.

## Family Security Index

1. **Housing** — affordable, adequate and stable.
2. **Income and work** — predictable hours and protection from permanent career penalties.
3. **Childcare** — available, reliable and backed up during closures.
4. **Care fairness** — time, mental load and career consequences shared fairly.
5. **Health** — fertility, pregnancy, birth and postpartum care.
6. **Relationships and community** — partnership, safety, time and social support.
7. **Access** — benefits and services without a bureaucracy trap.

## First policy tests

Priority A means a problem is visible and a bounded pilot appears plausible. It does **not** mean the intervention is proven to increase fertility.

- Childcare Reliability Guarantee;
- Equal-Care Parental Leave Test;
- Family Housing Access Pilot;
- Pregnancy and Parenthood Career Protection.

Additional scoped tests cover automatic family benefits, fertility care, postpartum health and support for a wanted second or third child.

## Safety principles

- no pressure to reproduce;
- no blame directed at women, migrants, childfree people or small families;
- no private fertility, pregnancy or health data through the public portal;
- no fertility-rate movement attributed to one intervention without credible evaluation;
- child welfare and bodily autonomy outrank demographic targets;
- migration, productivity and social-system adaptation remain part of demographic policy.
