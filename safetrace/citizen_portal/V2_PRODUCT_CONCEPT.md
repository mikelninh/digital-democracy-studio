# SafeTrace v2.0 — Civic Evidence Network

**Working vision:** Many citizens can investigate public systems together without creating a rumour network, automated accusation engine or uncontrolled evidence dump.

v1.0 proves that one public-source case can move safely from observation to evidence, reply, action and measured follow-up. v2.0 makes that process reusable across cities, Länder, newsrooms, universities, civil-society groups and qualified public bodies.

## The v2.0 promise

A citizen, local group or partner organisation can open a bounded public-source investigation, reuse a shared evidence standard, compare jurisdictions, challenge findings and coordinate a measurable improvement mission — while every claim, permission, review and correction remains attributable and auditable.

## What changes from v1.0

### v1.0

- one central public portal;
- maximum five active investigations;
- public sources only;
- human-controlled publication;
- case-level action and impact contracts;
- Germany-first seasons and portfolio management.

### v2.0

- a **federated network of local SafeTrace Labs**;
- shared but independently governed case and evidence standards;
- cross-city and cross-Land comparisons;
- qualified partner hand-offs for sensitive material;
- public APIs and reusable TRACE components;
- multi-party Impact Missions with explicit commitments;
- transparent reputation based on review quality, corrections and completed work — never outrage or popularity.

# Product architecture

## 1. Local Labs

A Local Lab may be operated by a newsroom, university, civic-tech group, NGO or qualified public-interest institution.

Each Lab receives:

- its own case board and capacity limits;
- role and permission controls;
- local legal and domain configuration;
- source retention and review tools;
- public correction and complaint process;
- federation controls defining what may be shared.

A Lab cannot inherit trust merely by installing the software. Public trust status requires named governance, conflict disclosures, review capacity and a completed safety assessment.

## 2. Federated Case Graph

Cases remain owned by their originating Lab, but public elements can be linked:

- shared research questions;
- identical data fields;
- related laws and institutions;
- reused primary sources;
- responses and corrections;
- comparable outcome metrics;
- superseding or contradictory evidence.

The graph must show whether a connection is:

- direct evidence;
- common source;
- shared actor;
- temporal sequence;
- analytical hypothesis;
- unresolved similarity.

Similarity is never causation.

## 3. TRACE Protocol as an open standard

v2.0 publishes versioned schemas for:

- Case Contract;
- Source Receipt;
- Claim Ledger;
- Counterevidence Record;
- Right-of-Reply Packet;
- Action Contract;
- Impact Contract;
- Correction and Appeal Record;
- Lab Governance Profile.

The protocol should work outside the SafeTrace interface and be usable through JSON, CSV, an API and accessible document exports.

## 4. AI Case Copilots

AI may assist with bounded tasks:

- extract claims from public documents;
- detect changed or superseded source versions;
- propose missing counterarguments;
- identify jurisdiction and likely responsible institutions;
- generate comparison tables;
- flag unsupported headlines or causal language;
- translate plain-language case summaries;
- prepare draft questions and data requests.

AI may not autonomously:

- declare guilt or corruption;
- publish a case;
- contact a named party;
- expose personal data;
- accept confidential evidence;
- make final legal, editorial or domain-review decisions.

Every AI output remains a proposal with model, prompt, input-source and reviewer receipt.

## 5. Qualified Sensitive-Source Bridge

SafeTrace itself should not become a generic leak box.

v2.0 can provide a visible hand-off layer to qualified partners that independently operate:

- encrypted intake;
- source protection;
- legal privilege or journalist-source safeguards where applicable;
- threat modelling;
- evidence authenticity procedures;
- emergency and law-enforcement protocols;
- retention and deletion policies.

The public portal receives only a minimal hand-off receipt such as `referred to qualified partner`, never the protected material or identity.

## 6. Impact Missions

Several actors can turn a verdict into a bounded improvement mission.

Example structure:

- verified problem;
- affected group;
- participating institutions;
- public commitments;
- intervention or data change;
- baseline and target;
- independent measurement owner;
- risk and unintended-consequence checks;
- 30/90/365-day results;
- contribution and attribution statement.

Missions can end as successful, partially successful, ineffective, harmful, inconclusive or superseded.

## 7. Civic Contribution System

Citizens earn no points for accusations or virality.

Useful contributions include:

- verifying an official document;
- identifying a superseding source;
- finding a counterexample;
- improving a definition;
- contributing comparable municipal data;
- translating a case accurately;
- completing an accessibility check;
- documenting an official response;
- discovering that a claim is wrong.

Public contributor profiles should display correction quality, completed reviews and methodological reliability, not a single influence score.

# Core v2.0 surfaces

## Network Home

- active Labs and their governance status;
- cross-region investigations;
- newly corrected or refuted claims;
- official responses and opened datasets;
- current Impact Missions;
- public health of the network: response rates, correction times, unresolved complaints and active-case capacity.

## Compare

A citizen can compare municipalities or Länder only when:

- definitions match;
- coverage is visible;
- missing data is explicit;
- case mix and context are included;
- uncertainty is shown;
- ranking is blocked when comparability is insufficient.

## Case Graph

A visual timeline and evidence graph showing:

- verified facts;
- contested claims;
- responses;
- corrections;
- responsible actors;
- action deadlines;
- impact checkpoints.

## Lab Console

- roles and permissions;
- active capacity;
- reviewer assignments;
- source receipts;
- incident and complaint queue;
- conflict declarations;
- federation and export controls.

## Public API and MCP

Read-only public endpoints for:

- published cases;
- claims and source receipts;
- correction histories;
- action and impact status;
- comparable public datasets;
- Lab governance profiles.

Write access must remain strongly permissioned and human-reviewed.

# Governance model

## Network constitution

Every participating Lab must accept common non-negotiables:

- no automated guilt decisions;
- no doxxing, hacking, impersonation or harassment;
- facts, allegations, hypotheses and verdicts remain separate;
- vulnerable identities and unnecessary personal data are protected;
- corrections remain visible;
- affected parties receive a meaningful reply route;
- financial and organisational conflicts are disclosed;
- active workload is capacity-limited;
- impact claims require evidence.

## Independence and funding

Every Lab publishes:

- legal operator;
- funding above a defined threshold;
- editorial and governance roles;
- reviewer conflicts;
- sponsor restrictions;
- current partners;
- annual transparency report.

Funders cannot remove findings, block corrections or receive unpublished personal data.

## Appeals and network sanctions

A Lab can be:

- warned;
- required to correct;
- temporarily prevented from federating new cases;
- removed from trusted-Lab status;
- disconnected from the network.

Grounds include fabricated evidence, hidden conflicts, repeated unsupported accusations, failure to correct, unsafe data handling or misrepresentation of review status.

# v2.0 release gates

## v1.0 proof completed first

- at least three end-to-end public-source cases;
- one case weakened, refuted or closed through counterevidence;
- complete claim-to-source traceability;
- independent editorial, domain and legal/privacy reviews;
- public 30/90-day impact report;
- citizen comprehension targets met.

## Federation proof

- three independently governed pilot Labs;
- at least two different operator types;
- shared Case Contract and Source Receipt interoperability;
- one cross-jurisdiction comparison completed;
- one correction propagated safely across Labs;
- federation disconnect and data-withdrawal procedure tested.

## Safety proof

- independent security assessment;
- permission and role tests;
- incident-response exercise;
- no confidential material stored in the public layer;
- qualified sensitive-source partner agreement;
- abuse, harassment and coordinated-manipulation tests;
- legal review for each pilot jurisdiction.

## Impact proof

- at least two Impact Missions with independent measurement owners;
- one mission showing measurable system change;
- one inconclusive or unsuccessful mission reported honestly;
- no conversion of reach metrics into unsupported outcome claims.

## Public usability proof

- 100 citizen tests across several regions;
- accessibility review and assistive-technology testing;
- plain-language comprehension in German and at least two additional languages;
- contributor and responding-party workflows tested;
- public API documentation and reproducible examples.

# Suggested path

## v1.1 — Reliable operations

Response tracking, reminders, source receipts, correction SLAs and three completed cases.

## v1.2 — Local comparison kits

Reusable request templates, data dictionaries and safe comparison rules for municipalities and Länder.

## v1.3 — Partner Lab prototype

Role-based Lab console, governance profile and export/import of Case Contracts.

## v1.5 — Federation sandbox

Two internal or closely partnered Labs exchange only synthetic and public-source cases.

## v1.7 — Qualified partner bridge

Test secure referral metadata without storing protected source material.

## v1.9 — Public federation candidate

Three real Labs, public governance profiles, independent audit and cross-Lab correction test.

## v2.0 — Civic Evidence Network

A production network that helps citizens and institutions move from overlooked public facts to fair investigation and measurable improvement — without turning civic participation into accusation theatre.

# North-star metrics

## Quality

Percentage of published material claims with reviewed Source Receipts and visible limitations.

## Fairness

Percentage of materially affected parties receiving a usable reply opportunity before publication.

## Action

Percentage of active cases with a responsible actor, exact ask, owner, deadline and stop condition.

## Impact

Percentage of completed cases producing verified Evidence, Accountability, System or Outcome Impact.

## Trust

Correction speed, appeal quality, refuted-case visibility and citizen comprehension — not follower count.

## Network health

Active capacity, reviewer load, unresolved complaints, Lab governance compliance and source-retention integrity.
