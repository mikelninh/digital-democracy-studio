# REAL-DE-MEAT-LOBBY-001

## Question

**Did the German meat industry get the policy change it wanted — and can the public record prove why?**

This is a real public-source investigation built from official German Bundestag lobbying disclosures, official legislative documents and clearly-labelled first-party association records.

## Analyst conclusion

- **Documented advocacy:** supported.
- **Policy alignment:** supported. PFG disclosed an objective to prevent the planned 1 August 2025 labelling obligation from taking effect before revision; Bundestag printed paper 21/327 later proposed moving the transition deadline to 1 March 2026.
- **Direct lobbying causation:** **not established**. The bill's executive footprint says interest representatives did not materially contribute to its content and gives an alternative account involving the Länder agriculture ministers and implementation concerns from affected economic actors.

The case therefore deliberately distinguishes **access/activity**, **alignment**, and **causation**.

## Trust stack

For every material finding, preserve:

1. **Conclusion** — the bounded analytical statement.
2. **Reason** — the derivation from the evidence.
3. **Evidence** — the record supporting the finding.
4. **Original source** — public URL where a reviewer can verify it independently.
5. **Authority** — official register, legislative document or labelled first-party source.
6. **Exact location** — section/page/record identifier.
7. **Gap / limitation** — what the evidence does not establish.
8. **Next move** — the highest-value evidence needed to strengthen or falsify the hypothesis.

## Critical boundary

Lobbying is a lawful and routine part of democratic policymaking. A registered lobbying project, association membership, meeting, statement or policy overlap must **not** be converted into an allegation of corruption, improper influence, quid-pro-quo conduct, party control or causation without additional evidence.

This case is designed to demonstrate that restraint.
