Where does Germany’s defence money actually go?

Follow one major Bundeswehr ammunition framework from public spending to Rheinmetall — while keeping framework ceiling, concrete orders, realised business, shareholder value and lobbying analytically separate.

Every material relationship opens the original public source.
01 · Current assessment

What does the evidence support right now?

The biggest analytical trap is turning the largest number on the page into money already spent — or turning lobbying into a procurement explanation without evidence.

Framework ceilingUp to €8.5bn

Rheinmetall disclosed an expanded Bundeswehr framework for 155mm artillery ammunition.

Initial call-off~€880m

The same disclosure identifies the first concrete call-off. This is much closer to committed business than the ceiling.

Lobbying caused awardNot proven

Rheinmetall and its industry association lobby on defence issues, but the selected public record does not establish procurement causation.

Current assessmentA very large procurement opportunity is documented. The evidence does not support translating the €8.5bn ceiling into “money paid”, assigning group profit to this one framework, or claiming lobbying caused the award.
02 · Why

Follow the money without inventing missing links.

This is a chain of institutions and disclosed amounts. It is not a claim that every euro in one box flows directly into the next.

Missing link

The selected sources do not identify the exact budget title and funding mix behind each ammunition call-off. We therefore do not draw a fake euro-for-euro line from the full defence budget to Rheinmetall.

04 · Gap → next move

What evidence would most change the assessment?

Best next evidence

The specific €25m+ parliamentary procurement file and subsequent call-off disclosures. Those can connect approval → amount → timing → delivery far more precisely.

What it would resolve

A call-off ledger could distinguish framework capacity from concrete orders, identify payer/recipient and reveal how much of the headline ceiling has actually become auditable procurement over time.

Next move
Build a call-off ledger, not a headline counter.

Track each concrete order separately: approval date, amount, payer, recipient country, delivery window, recognised revenue and source.
