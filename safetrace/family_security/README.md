# SafeTrace Family Security Lab

## Case

**CAND-051 — Der Kinderwunsch im Wartestand**

### Bounded question

Which economic, time, health and relationship insecurities prevent people in Germany from realising their desired family size, and which measures help without pressuring women or families?

## Why this framing

The project does not set a state fertility target. It measures reproductive agency:

- people who want children can build the family they want;
- people who do not want children are respected;
- unintended pregnancies are prevented;
- pregnancy and birth are safe;
- parenthood does not impose avoidable, gendered economic or health damage.

## Verified starting facts

- Germany's total fertility rate was 1.32 in 2025.
- 654,241 children were born in 2025, the lowest post-war total.
- Preliminary births in January–April 2026 remained 2.5% below the same period in 2025.
- UN projections do not show human extinction this century: world population is projected to peak near 10.3 billion in the mid-2080s and remain near 10.2 billion in 2100.
- UNFPA's 2025 14-country survey found a substantial gap between desired and expected family size and identified financial, housing, employment, childcare and future-security barriers.
- OECD evidence points to housing costs, labour-market security, childcare, leave and gender equality as relevant conditions, while warning that no simple policy restores replacement fertility.

## Family Security Index v0.1

1. affordable and adequate housing;
2. predictable income and working time;
3. available and reliable childcare, including closure risk;
4. fair distribution of care and career consequences;
5. reproductive, maternity and postpartum healthcare;
6. partnership, time, community and safety;
7. simple access to family benefits and services.

## Policy experiments to compare

SafeTrace should not recommend these before defining baselines, target groups, cost and outcome measures.

- family-housing access or guarantees;
- childcare reliability guarantees and emergency backup care;
- parental-leave designs that increase fathers' care share without cutting family security;
- protection against pregnancy and parenthood career penalties;
- simpler and more automatic family-benefit access;
- affordable fertility diagnostics and treatment with equal eligibility rules;
- postpartum and maternal mental-health access;
- local family-support networks and time infrastructure;
- targeted support for second and third wanted children;
- long-term adaptation through migration, productivity and healthy working lives.

## First data sprint

## Capacity boundary

This is a scoped case, not a sixth active investigation. It can enter the active portfolio only after a current active case closes or pauses and the portfolio review documents the replacement decision.

## Safety and fairness

- no pressure to reproduce;
- no blame directed at women, migrants, childfree people or individual families;
- no private fertility, pregnancy or health records through the public portal;
- no claim that a correlation proves a policy caused births;
- no demographic argument overrides bodily autonomy or child welfare.
