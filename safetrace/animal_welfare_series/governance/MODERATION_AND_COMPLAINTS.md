# Moderation, complaints and appeals

## Channels

The public alpha offers separate GitHub forms for:

1. factual correction;
2. complaint or harm concern;
3. public-source case proposal.

Do not place confidential material, private addresses, medical records, witness identities or unpublished allegations in GitHub.

## Triage

Every submission receives one visible category:

- `factual_correction`;
- `privacy_or_safety`;
- `fairness_or_context`;
- `source_quality`;
- `accessibility`;
- `abuse_or_harassment`;
- `out_of_scope`.

Target acknowledgement: three working days. This is a service target, not a legal deadline.

## Immediate protective actions

A maintainer may temporarily hide, unpublish or freeze a claim when there is a credible risk involving:

- wrong identity;
- private or sensitive data;
- imminent harm;
- fabricated evidence;
- material quotation or source error;
- active legal restriction.

Temporary action is not an admission that the complaint is correct.

## Decision process

1. Preserve the challenged version and complaint receipt.
2. Separate factual, legal, privacy, fairness and policy questions.
3. Assign a reviewer who did not author the challenged claim where possible.
4. Invite a response from the original author.
5. Decide: no change, clarification, correction, retraction, temporary hold or escalation.
6. Publish a short rationale unless doing so creates additional harm.
7. Keep material corrections visible.

## Appeal

A complainant may request one appeal based on:

- overlooked evidence;
- conflict of interest;
- procedural failure;
- disproportionate remedy.

The appeal must be reviewed by someone other than the initial decision-maker.

## Abuse controls

Submissions may be closed for threats, doxxing, discriminatory abuse, spam, repeated unsupported accusations or attempts to weaponise the system against private individuals.
